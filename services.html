01 · Most common

Lower back pain

About four in ten people who book with us come in for this. Usually it is mechanical — a joint that is not moving well, muscle guarding around it, and a habit that keeps reloading it. Sometimes it started with a lift; more often it started with nothing in particular.

What we assess: lumbar and hip range, single-leg control, straight-leg raise, and the neurological screen. We are specifically ruling out the small number of causes that need someone other than us.

What we do: spinal manipulation or mobilisation depending on how irritable you are, soft-tissue work through the surrounding muscle, and two exercises you actually have time to do.

02

Neck pain & desk strain

Stiffness on one side, a restricted turn when reversing the car, an ache that builds through the afternoon. Almost always the mid-back is part of it, which is why treating only the neck tends not to hold.

What we assess: cervical range in all six directions, thoracic mobility, shoulder-blade control, and how your workstation is actually set up on a normal working day.

What we do: cervical and thoracic mobilisation, targeted soft-tissue work, and a short set of movement breaks tied to something you already do hourly.

Median 4–5 visitsDesk assessment included

03

Sciatica & nerve pain

Pain, pins and needles or weakness travelling below the knee. This is the one where the assessment matters most, because “sciatica” describes a symptom, not a cause, and the causes are treated differently.

What we assess: a full neurological screen — reflexes, myotomes, dermatomes — plus nerve tension testing and directional preference. We will refer you for imaging if the findings warrant it, and we will tell you why.

What we do: nerve mobilisation, directional loading, and joint work above and below the involved level. Progress is measured by where the pain stops travelling to, not by how it feels on the day.

Median 6–8 visitsNeurological screen included

04

Tension headaches

Headaches that start at the base of the skull and wrap forward, often one-sided, often worse late in the day. When they are coming from the upper neck, they respond well. When they are not, we say so.

What we assess: upper cervical joint mobility, the flexion-rotation test, jaw and breathing pattern, and a two-week headache diary if you can keep one.

What we do: upper cervical mobilisation, sub-occipital release, and deep neck flexor retraining. We track headache-days per month. It is a blunter number than a pain score and it is the one patients actually notice.

Median 5 visitsTracked in headache-days

05

Sports & running injuries

Achilles, ITB, shin pain, hamstring niggles, shoulder pain in overhead sport. The injury is usually the last link in a chain that starts with a training-load change you did not think counted.

What we assess: the injured tissue, the joint above and below, and your last six weeks of training volume. Dr Okonkwo runs this caseload and works with two local running clubs.

What we do: soft-tissue work including Graston where it helps, joint mobilisation, and a loading programme that keeps you training rather than resting completely.

Median 3–6 visitsTraining-load reviewDr Okonkwo

06

Posture & everyday strain

Carrying a child on one hip, a bag on one shoulder, long drives, the first year of parenthood. Not an injury so much as an accumulation — which means the fix is partly treatment and partly logistics.

What we assess: loaded and unloaded posture, hip and thoracic mobility, and the specific daily task that keeps aggravating it.

What we do: mobilisation through the stiff segments, strengthening for what is working too hard, and honest advice about the carrying habit. Pregnancy and postnatal care sits here too, with Dr Reyes.
